Why now

The customer journey has a new front door — and it isn't a browser tab.

For twenty years, "customer-ready" meant human-ready: fast pages, clear copy, a checkout that converts. That's no longer the whole job. A growing share of discovery, research, and transactions now happens through AI agents acting on a person's behalf — reading your site, your data, and your APIs, then deciding what to tell the human who's actually paying.

⚡

It's moving faster than the last platform shift

Mobile took a decade to force redesigns. Agents are being wired into search, browsers, and operating systems in real time — the window to adapt deliberately, instead of scrambling later, is now.

🕳️

If an agent can't parse you, you don't exist

An agent that can't extract clean answers, prices, or actions from your site simply routes around it — straight to a competitor it can understand. There's no "página no encontrada" moment to notice; you're just absent.

🏁

Early movers set the defaults

The companies that structure their data and open clean interfaces first become the sources agents trust and cite by default. Being second here doesn't mean catching up — it means being recommended less, quietly, forever.

The framework

The agentic customer journey has two halves. Most companies have prepared for neither.

Agents don't experience your brand the way people do. They arrive to answer a question, and they act on what they find. AgentSee builds readiness across both stages of that journey.

01

Discovery

Getting found, cited, and recommended by AI agents and answer engines

  • AEO — Answer Engine Optimization Structuring your content so agents can extract accurate, quotable answers instead of guessing.
  • GEO — Generative Engine Optimization Earning citation and recommendation inside AI-generated answers, not just a blue link on page one.
02

Usage

Letting agents actually transact, compare, and complete tasks on your platform

  • Structured data Schema.org markup and clean data models so product, pricing, and policy facts are machine-legible.
  • APIs & MCP Exposing capabilities through the Model Context Protocol so agents can act, not just read.
  • WebMCP Bringing agent-native interaction directly into the browser experience of your own website.
